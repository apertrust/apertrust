# 0099. The zoomed view: a detail pipeline beside the fitted one

## Context

The Viewer only fits the photo. Zooming it, to 100% above all, where the screen shows what the export will be (principle 2), needs the engine to render part of the photo at a larger scale. A 61 MP photo at full resolution is about 1 GB a buffer and 27 to 29 ms a pass on the M4 and the Radeon 780M, so the engine cannot render the whole photo at 100% on every change ([0012](0012-planning-regions-scale-and-cache-points.md)). The scopes, the colour pickers and the readouts must give the same values at every zoom ([0019](0019-scopes-and-readouts-counted-on-the-gpu.md), [0079](0079-colour-pickers-one-api-for-tools-and-the-colour-picker.md)), and whole-image work runs over the whole image at the pipe's scale, never over a visible region ([0015](0015-whole-image-passes.md), [0063](0063-departures-from-darktable-and-views-below-full-scale.md)). How does the engine render a zoomed view, how does a client ask for one, and what does a frame carry?

## Decision

**Two pipelines while zoomed.** The fitted viewport pipeline (`Pipe::Viewport`) keeps running as it does at Fit, and a detail pipeline renders only the visible region, with a margin, at the zoom's scale, never above 1. At Fit the fitted pipeline alone renders.

- **The fitted pipeline** stays the one the scopes, the colour pickers (the tools' and the Colour picker's frame samples), the readouts and the live photo's rendered thumbnails read, so a pick or a scope gives the same values at every zoom. It is the small whole-image pipe decisions 19 and 79 left open. It renders on every change of the edit while zoomed, as darktable's preview pipe does.
- **The detail pipeline** is a plan of a new pipe kind, `Pipe::Detail { region, scale }`: `region` in the output image's pixels at `scale`, after any geometry. Up to its start it is planned as an export tile; after it, as the viewport: cache points, an edit cache, whole-image results keyed apart, `PipeKind::Viewport`. Existing viewport and export plans keep their bytes and keys.
- **Its source** is demosaic's full-resolution cache point, borrowed from the fitted pipeline in place by key, as a still borrows the live pipe's cached images (`Pipeline::still`). Where the fitted viewport is tiled (lavapipe above 8.4 MP, any GPU over the budget) and holds no full-resolution image, the detail pipeline starts from the raw stage's output it keeps, copying the mosaic's region, aligned to the colour pattern and grown by demosaic's reach, and demosaicing it, as an export tile does: below 1 in tiles of the region where it passes the binding, as the fitted viewport's start is, and at 1 with its margin shrunk to fit the binding, as the margin's rule below says ([0014](0014-row-strips-for-images-over-one-binding.md)).
- **At scale 1** its first operation is the engine's `region` copy of the source. **Between Fit and 1** (Fill, 50%, a pinch) it is the viewport's box downscale of that region, with the integer footprints of the whole image at that scale, so its pixels are exactly the fitted viewport's at that scale wherever a fitted viewport has that output's size; every later operation runs at that scale, its lengths scaled as decisions 15 and 63 say. **Above 1** the engine renders at 1: the client enlarges that frame, smooth between 100% and 200% and square pixels from 200%, as darktable pixel-doubles from 200%. **Small**, half of Fit, is the fitted frame drawn at half by the client, an exact 2:1 box; the engine renders nothing more for it.
- **Whole-image results** in the detail pipeline cover the whole image at its scale, never the region ([0015](0015-whole-image-passes.md)): a first round over the whole image, as an export's, the operations that opt in (`Operation::tiles_whole_image_passes`) in tiles from the source, the others (local contrast, filmic rgb's reconstruction) whole with every operation before them. The result stays in the detail pipeline's scratch under the key the untiled plan at that scale gives it, with no region in it, so a pan, an edit after the operation and a move between 100% and 1600% keep it. Haze removal's full-resolution part is borrowed from the fitted pipeline by its key, never estimated twice; at 100%, where the fitted pipeline holds none, as in a tiled viewport or a plan with geometry, where it estimates at its own scale ([0015](0015-whole-image-passes.md)), the detail pipeline computes the estimate at full resolution in its own round. Below 100%: upright, with an estimate to borrow, the detail borrows it alone and its round runs `plane_downscale` to the detail's scale; upright, with none to borrow, its round estimates at full resolution first; with geometry after haze removal, or from a tiled viewport's mosaic, it estimates at the detail's scale, as the fitted viewport at that scale does. **A drag upstream of such an operation,** or of its own settings its whole-image passes read (the tone equalizer's mask), would rerun its round on every change: on the M4 at 100%, 109.7 to 118.5 ms a frame from the command with the tone equalizer, about 0.4 s with local contrast. During a gesture (`SetControl { done: false }` changes, [history.md](../history.md)) whose change would rerun a round of the detail, the session renders the fitted pipeline alone and leaves the detail as it is, unpublished: the frame is the fitted one, placed as at Fit and marked `detail_pending`, and the client draws it enlarged where the detail would be, blurred only while the slider moves, with no Rendering… for the drag. The gesture's end (`done: true`), its cancel (`CancelGesture`) or any other command that keeps the photo open, a `View` among them, which ends no gesture, renders the detail with its rounds, exact as every detail frame is. There is no threshold: every gesture whose change would rerun a round takes the fitted frame, whatever the round costs; one that reruns none (a control after the operation, an edit with no whole-image operation, haze removal's estimate borrowed whole at 100%) render the detail on every change, and a pan renders as it does with no drag.
- **The margin.** The rendered region is the visible rectangle grown to darktable's `darkroom/ui/anticipate_move`, 1.2 times the view, snapped outward to a 64-pixel grid at the scale and held at a constant size, moved rather than shrunk at the photo's edges, so a re-render updates the plan in place. The setting is `viewer.anticipate_move`, 1.0 to 2.0, default 1.2, with no page. A view at the rendered region's scale whose visible rectangle stays inside the region renders nothing; one that leaves it, or one at another scale, renders the region at its own scale round its new centre; edits keep the region. On a GPU that binds less, the margin shrinks to fit the binding before the zoom is refused, as the fitted viewport is above 8.4 MP on lavapipe.
- **Caches.** The detail pipeline's start is a cache point keyed by its source's key, its region and its scale; it has its own edit cache and its whole-image results as above. At Fit it is kept but not rendered, freed first where it would take the fitted pipeline past the budget, and freed with the photo.
- **One turn a frame** ([0018](0018-one-submit-gate-for-the-engine-and-the-window.md)): a pan or a zoom records the detail pipeline's passes alone. **The detail first:** a change's frame records the detail's passes alone and goes out with no overview; the fitted pipeline's passes follow in a frame of their own, which carries the overview, as soon as no command waits. They join the detail's in one encoder and one submission (`Pipeline::record`), the fitted passes first, where the detail reads what they write (its source after a raw-stage change, a whole-image result it borrows that runs again), where a tool's picker measures in them, and once the fitted frame has followed a drag for 100 ms, so the scopes and the overview follow it about ten times a second.
- **One budget** for the session: the detail pipeline's buffers, about 88 MB each at 2400×1600 with the margin, about 530 MB in all with its display buffers, and its whole-image scratch are counted beside the fitted pipeline's. Where the detail takes them past the budget, the fitted pipeline's full-resolution image gives way first ([0012](0012-planning-regions-scale-and-cache-points.md)), the detail pipeline then starting from the raw stage's output; then the rounds' tiles shrink to what both pipelines leave; the rest is logged. A fitted pipeline that passes the budget alone, as at Fit, keeps its plan, and one that keeps a whole-image result at full resolution, haze removal's estimate, keeps its full-resolution image, so the scopes and pickers read the same at every zoom.

**The command.** `Command::View(View { scale, centre })`, view state as `Resize` and `Hide` are: never a command on the edit, so it ends no gesture, is taken while the photo opens and while it is open for viewing only, and coalesces with the commands of its batch.

- `scale` is a `ViewScale`: `Small`, `Fit`, `Fill` or `Factor(f32)`, 1.0 being 100%, one photo pixel per physical pixel, a factor clamped to Fit and 16. `Small` is half of Fit; it is part of the shared view like the others, the engine rendering the fitted frame for it and its `Placement` showing it at half.
- `centre` is a point in the photo's coordinates, 0 to 1 across the photo as export renders it, the pickers' own ([0079](0079-colour-pickers-one-api-for-tools-and-the-colour-picker.md)), so a script or an MCP tool names a view without knowing the window.
- The engine places the view from it and the last `Resize`'s bounds through one pure function the client uses too, `apertrust_engine::view::place(extent, bounds, &view) -> Placement`: the scale shown, the scale rendered (at most 1), the visible rectangle and the centre clamped so the view never passes the photo's edges. A resize at Fill stays Fill without a round trip.
- The session keeps the view across photos, until a client sends another; another photo's frame takes the same view, its centre clamped.
- **The clamp is the session's once a photo is live.** The session holds the view as asked and places it on each photo; the clamp is written back into that view as the photo goes live and at every placement while it is live, a `View` a client sends and an edit that turns or crops the photo included. While the photo is live the view a client sends wins, sent again after a resize or a change of extent, and the session commits its clamp, so the photo after carries the centre the session holds as the photo is left: the last view asked, clamped at the photo's latest placement, a resize or a crop after it included, and mapped through its turns, a `View` in the batch that leaves the photo, never placed on it, going on as asked. A turn or a flip of the live photo maps the view the session holds onto the same point of the scene, through the one function a client maps its own centre with (`view::reoriented`). An opening photo's placement is provisional, so a photo that fails to open leaves the view as asked, a `View` sent while it opened included.
- **One view per session,** as `Resize`'s bounds are: the session renders one view for its window (one session per window, [0037](0037-one-view-the-photos-tab.md)), so every client of a session shares it and the last `View` any client sends wins. Every client learns it from `Event::View(ViewState { through, view, placement })`, a fact (topic `view`, [0059](0059-one-event-system.md)): the view as the session places it, its centre clamped, and its `Placement`. It is sent once in each batch whose commands changed it or its placement (a `View`, a `Resize`, an `Open` of a photo of another extent, an edit that changes the extent), after the batch's `Values` and before its `Frame`, and to a client that syncs mid-session; a pan inside the rendered region, which renders no frame, still sends it, so a second client never shows an old view.

**The frame.** `Frame` gains:

- `place: FramePlace { scale, origin, extent }`: the scale the frame was rendered at, its corner in the output image at that scale, and the output image at full resolution. At Fit the scale is the fitted one and the origin (0, 0).
- `overview: Option<Overview { buffer, width, height }>`: the fitted pipeline's frame while zoomed past Fit, `None` at Fit and in a detail frame whose fitted frame follows (One turn a frame), a later `Frame` carrying it, the next one, of the same commands, where no command comes first: a frame never shows the fitted frame of another edit than its detail's. A client draws it, enlarged to the zoom, wherever the detail frame does not reach yet, as darktable draws its preview pipe's image while its full pipe catches up.
- `detail_pending: bool`: zoomed past Fit, the detail left pending by a drag upstream of a whole-image operation (Whole-image results): the frame is the fitted pipeline's, its `place` the fitted one, with no overview, and the client draws it enlarged to the zoom; `false` otherwise.
- `buffer` is the image shown: the detail pipeline's while zoomed, the fitted one's at Fit and while the detail is pending. Each has its own pair of display buffers, written alternately ([0017](0017-threads-and-the-engines-session.md)).
- The scopes count, and the Colour picker samples, the fitted frame of the edit the frame shows: the overview's buffer while zoomed, in the frame that carries it, and the frame's at Fit and while the detail is pending.

## Options considered

- **One pipeline at the zoom's scale over the whole image,** panned by the client: at 100% a 61 MP photo is 1 GB a buffer and 27 to 29 ms a pass, about 5 GB in all, as decision 12 found; and the scopes would change with the zoom.
- **Every zoom past Fit rendered at 100% for its region and downscaled at the end:** exact, but Fill on a 61 MP photo renders about 43 MP at full resolution on every change.
- **The fitted frame enlarged for scales between Fit and 100%:** not the processing at that scale.
- **The pipeline itself upsampled between 100% and 200%,** as darktable's darkroom pipe is: up to four times the pixels on every change, and a render that is no longer the export's. Rendering at most at 1:1 and enlarging the frame is what RawTherapee, ART, vkdt, RapidRAW, LightCraft and CalibRaw do, and Ansel, whose `src/iop/finalscale.c` runs the pipeline at most at 100% for pixel-level accuracy and consistency with exports; darktable itself pixel-doubles from 200%.
- **One pipeline with two outputs,** a branch in the plan: a graph in the planner and the executor before layers need one. **A shared source object** holding the raw stage and demosaic for every pipeline: cleaner, kept for when zoomed stills need it, but a refactor of keys and rebuilds today.
- **`Pipe::Viewport` generalised to a scale and a region:** churns every viewport plan and its fingerprints for the same behaviour.
- **The client sending the visible rectangle, or the region in pixels:** a script would need the window's size, and the client and the engine could disagree on the clamping and the margin.
- **A separate `Event::Overview`:** two facts that can carry different `through` for a turn.
- **A view per client,** each client its own region rendered: one session renders for one window, and an assistant on the app's session should see and move what the person sees. **No view event,** clients learning the view from frames alone: a pan inside the rendered region sends no frame.
- **Exactly the visible region:** a render, about 11 ms on the M4, on every step of a pan. **A tile cache,** as maps keep: every edit invalidates every tile, and tiles overlap by their reach.
- **A small preview pipe of its own,** as darktable's: a third size whose values would differ from the fitted view's.
- **Both pipelines in one submission on every change,** published together: the detail's frame waits for the fitted passes, 21 to 22 ms from the command on the M4 at 100% against 14 ms with the detail first. **The overview of the edit before beside the new detail:** a frame showing two edits, and scopes that disagree with it.
- **For a drag upstream of a whole-image operation:** the cost accepted, 109.7 to 118.5 ms a frame with the tone equalizer and about 0.4 s with local contrast at 100% on the M4; the rounds stepped a submission at a time across turns, the last detail shown meanwhile; the result kept from before the drag, sharp but with the operations after the slider on a stale result until the release; the fitted frame only where the round's measured cost passes a threshold, a second rule for drags that cost little either way.

## Consequences

- The 100% frame equals the same region of the export bit for bit; a detail frame at a scale below 1 equals the same region of the fitted viewport at that scale wherever a fitted viewport has that output's size; the scopes and a pick are the same at Fit and at 100%; a drag whose change would rerun a whole-image round runs none until it ends or another command comes, and the detail its end renders is the same exact frame. Each is a test.
- Measured on the M4, the 7R V's 61 MP photo at 2400×1600 (engine.md, The zoomed view, step 7): an exposure change at 100% 13.8 to 14.3 ms from its command to the detail's frame, 9.6 ms of GPU time, against 10.0 to 10.5 ms at Fit and 21.3 to 22.3 ms with both pipelines in one frame, the fitted frame following at 22.7 to 23.5 ms; entering 100% 25 to 27 ms; a pan past the margin 14 ms. With the tone equalizer, an exposure drag upstream of its round shows the fitted frame in 12.8 to 13.8 ms a change, as at Fit, where rerunning the round each change would cost 109.7 to 118.5 ms at 100% and 64.1 to 65.4 ms at 50%; the drag's end renders the detail in 0.10 to 0.17 s at 100%, its round and passes 54 to 57 ms of GPU time, and 62 to 87 ms at 50%. On lavapipe and WARP a change's detail takes 0.43 s and 1.49 s; the Radeon 780M's are to come.
- On a software GPU the whole-image rounds meet the export's refusals ([0007](0007-a-software-gpu-instead-of-a-cpu-path.md)).
- Planned in seven steps (engine.md, Pipes, The zoomed view): the command and the frame's place; the detail pipeline at 100%; whole-image results at 100%; pans; scales between Fit and 100%; a tiled viewport; timings, the detail first and the budget. All seven are built.
- Several photos (Phase 3) zoom their stills with the same pipe kind, each cell's region from its own source.
